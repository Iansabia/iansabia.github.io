Medtronic

Engineering Intern, Quality Systems · Summer 2026 · North Haven, CT

A surgical device plant kept its quality and production records in five separate systems with no governed way to query across them, so the quality team had effectively become the site’s manual lookup service. I interviewed them to find where the time was actually going, then built a governed semantic layer over the ERP and put a conversational agent on top of it.

I shipped it into Microsoft Teams instead of building an app, because Teams was already where the plant worked and adoption then required no new habit. After presenting to site leadership it was released across operations.

~16.9MERP rows modeled
15 min → 1record lookup time
5 → 1systems, one place to ask
15agent tools shipped

The part I think about most is what made it accurate. It was not the model. It was roughly 35,000 characters of domain rules written by hand: never sum production receipts across bill-of-material levels, never average an average, the fiscal year starts in May. And when I audited the agent against the source system, the real defects were upstream in the data, not in the answers.

Complexity Is Not an Edge

ML research · Boston University, DS340

We asked whether more model complexity buys more predictive edge on prediction-market price gaps, and trained four tiers to find out: linear regression and XGBoost, then GRU/LSTM/TFT, then PPO, then PPO with an autoencoder.

It does not. Linear regression won. It reached a 0.501 per-trade Sharpe at +15.0 bps of alpha while the reinforcement-learning model collapsed to +0.5 bps, roughly thirty times less edge for a great deal more compute. We re-ran every claim on a leakage-free split before publishing rather than tuning until the result looked better, and wrote it up as a 29-page paper.

About

What I am actually interested in.

Trust is the hard part, not intelligence.

Every system I have built recently has been easy to make impressive in a demo and hard to make honest in production. The agent at Medtronic was accurate because of the rules around it, not the model inside it. The research project was worth something because we published the result we did not want.

So the questions I care about are the unglamorous ones. How do you know a system is right? What should it do when it is unsure? Who is accountable for the data underneath it? That is why I am studying data science and taking AI ethics and technology law alongside it, and why I want to work on data platforms rather than on top of them.

I am a junior at Boston University in the Faculty of Computing & Data Sciences, graduating in Spring 2028, with a minor in philosophy. I spend the rest of my time on chess, skiing, and golf, which is how the startup started.
