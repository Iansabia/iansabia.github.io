Medtronic · North Haven, CT

Five systems, one question

A medical device plant kept its quality records in five separate systems. Every time someone had a question, the quality team dug through them by hand.

I sat with the team first to learn what they actually used. Then I built one Snowflake database across all five, with an AI agent on top that lives in Microsoft Teams, where the plant already works. After I presented it to site leadership, it rolled out across the site.

The model was the easy part. What made it accurate was the boring stuff underneath: clean joins, written rules, and sending unsure matches to a human instead of guessing.

Continuum · Co-Founder

The caddy you already own

An AI caddy that runs on your phone. It tracks your shots from ordinary video, gives swing feedback, and tells you where to aim based on where you've been missing.

We started out building software for indoor simulators. About 70 conversations with golfers moved us onto the course instead. I lead product and ML.

We test everything against hand labels before we trust it. When SAM 3.1 beat our own ball tracker on the same 91 hand-labeled frames, in a test that cost under $1, we switched.

NVIDIA Physical AI challenge · solo

Teaching a car to explain itself

NVIDIA's challenge asks a self-driving model to explain why the car makes its decision in unusual scenes. A judge model grades each explanation against human labels.

I took NVIDIA's own driving model, Alpamayo 1.5, trained it further on the task, and got it running on a MacBook with a rewrite that makes it answer 4 to 7× faster.

Try it below: make the call on four real dashcam clips, then see what my model said. The contest closed on Oct 31.
